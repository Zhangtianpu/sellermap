When an overseas buyer wants distribution rights: 12 questions for e-bike traders

Official U.S. and UK export guidance is translated into an e-bike distributor scorecard covering channel model, territory, stock, service, financial evidence and market plans before exclusivity is discussed.

Published: 2026-11-23Verified: 2026-11-23View primary source ↗

Do not send an exclusive agreement yet: turn interest into verifiable capability

An email saying, “We want to be your exclusive distributor,” proves willingness to talk—not sales reach, inventory capacity, after-sales competence, financial strength or brand-building ability. E-bikes also require assembly support, diagnostics, spare parts, battery storage and transport discipline, and warranty response, so the size of a first order is not the only signal of channel value.

Start by identifying the relationship. UK government export guidance describes agents as intermediaries who generate sales for commission without holding stock, while distributors typically buy products, hold inventory and manage local logistics; the supplier gives up some direct control over customers, pricing and marketing. Different roles require different questions, pricing and contractual responsibilities.

Questions 1–6: can the partner cover the market and support the product?

Ask: (1) how many salespeople, stores, dealers and business accounts are active; (2) which cities and customer segments are actually covered; (3) which brands are represented and whether conflicts exist; (4) what warehouse capacity, stock system and safety-stock plan are available; (5) whether trained technicians, diagnostic tools and a warranty workflow exist; and (6) whether the partner will stock high-frequency parts such as batteries, controllers, displays and brake pads and complete product and safety training.

The U.S. International Trade Administration checklist separately assesses sales force, record, territory, product mix, warehousing, inventory control, service equipment, training and repair-part inventory. An e-bike exporter can turn these general questions into evidence requests: a coverage map, team list, warehouse or workshop video, represented-brand list, spare-parts plan and sample warranty ticket.

Questions 7–12: is the promise supported by evidence and a plan?

Ask: (7) can registration, owners and principal officers be verified; (8) can trade and bank references be supplied; (9) what happened to relevant-category sales over the past three to five years and which records support the figures; (10) what is the partner's view of the market, competitors, price bands and entry requirements; (11) which accounts, activities and content will be pursued in the first 90 days; and (12) what supplier support is requested and which inventory, service, marketing and reporting responsibilities will the partner accept?

The U.S. International Trade Administration advises investigating candidates before agreement, requesting officer backgrounds, product-introduction methods, and trade and bank references, and considering at least two supporting business or credit reports from different sources. Search results may provide leads, but the same guidance warns that online information may be outdated or inaccurate. Important conclusions should be cross-checked against registries, third-party reports, references, banks or professional advisers.

Let a pilot earn exclusivity instead of granting exclusivity for a trial

After basic verification, use a time-limited pilot with defined territory and product scope. One practical framework is: complete training, demo units and a target-account list within 30 days; complete first visits, local content and an after-sales drill by day 60; and review qualified leads, quotations, orders, stock and service response at day 90. This is a management recommendation derived from the official evaluation dimensions, not a law or universal industry standard.

Tie exclusivity to measurable milestones. Minimum purchasing may be one condition, but channel coverage, demo units, spare-parts availability, technician training, lead-feedback time and warranty response can matter too. A qualified lawyer in the target market should review the final agency or distribution agreement, including territory, term, trademark use, pricing boundaries, data, anti-bribery, termination and after-sales obligations.

For e-bike traders: build a reusable scorecard in one week

On day one, define agent and distributor roles. On day two, turn the 12 questions into a form with fields for answer, evidence, verification gap, owner and due date. Use days three and four to collect registration, brand, customer, warehouse, service, financial and market-plan evidence; day five for a video tour of stock and service facilities; day six for reference calls and public-record checks; and day seven for a continue, pilot or stop decision.

A scorecard may group market coverage, commercial credibility, inventory and finance, technical service and brand investment, but a total score must not hide red flags. Unverifiable identity, refusal to provide references, demands for immediate national exclusivity, unsupported customer claims, or no service capability for a maintenance-intensive product should trigger separate review. Date the evidence and refresh it after appointment instead of treating due diligence as a one-time form.
